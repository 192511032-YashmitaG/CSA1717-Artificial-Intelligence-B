TASK 4 – BEST, AVERAGE AND WORST CASES

BFS
Best case: the goal is the start node, so only one node is generated. Average case: the goal is found partway through the shallowest goal level; nodes before it in queue order are expanded. Worst case: the goal is the last node at depth d or is unreachable; nearly every node through depth d, or the whole reachable graph, is processed. The queue can become large because an entire breadth level may be waiting.

Iterative DFS
Best case: the goal is the first successor explored, requiring very few stack operations. Average case: the goal is found after exploring part of the reachable depth-first traversal. Worst case: the goal is last in the traversal order or unreachable; all reachable vertices and edges are examined. The stack is often smaller than BFS's frontier, but the visited and parent structures still grow with V.

Recursive IDDFS
Best case: the goal is the start node and the first limit succeeds. Average case: the goal is found after several limits and part of the final depth-limited traversal. Worst case: the goal is at depth d and is last in the final depth-first order, or is unreachable; repeated calls at earlier limits add overhead. The recursive call stack is bounded by the current depth limit, but total calls increase with branching factor and solution depth.

For a graph with duplicates, the visited/on-path policy affects the counts. For the experiment, a deterministic tree avoids duplicate paths so the effect of the three search orders can be isolated.
